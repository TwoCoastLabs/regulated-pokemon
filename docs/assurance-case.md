# Assurance case: what zero means

Regulated Pokémon tests a bounded claim: **within a declared assurance
envelope, an unsupported governed claim or unauthorized action must be
uncommittable.** This is stronger than observing no errors in a sample and
narrower than claiming universal truth. The distinction is the project's
contract with its reader.

The model is an untrusted proposer. A deterministic kernel is the only
component allowed to commit a governed claim or action, and it does so only
after re-deriving the result from recorded, versioned inputs. Outside that
path, the system asks, routes, abstains or clearly separates an uncertified
surface. A refusal is a valid outcome; a silent guess is not.

## The assurance envelope

Every reported zero is qualified by the envelope in which it was measured:

- **knowledge authority and version** — the designated upstreams, certified
  snapshot digest, provenance, fidelity declarations and effective period;
- **supported semantics** — the closed entity, fact, question, claim, roster,
  comparison, recommendation and action vocabularies;
- **policy** — the exact Accord pack, locale, eligibility gates, required
  disclosures and display floors;
- **principal and context** — the authenticated channel, bound subject,
  material profile, jurisdiction or game version, and validity window;
- **commit surface** — the renderer, independent DOM verification, transport
  boundary and action gateway covered by the proof chain; and
- **implementation** — the kernel, loaders, certification pipeline and replay
  code at the version under test.

An assertion outside that envelope is not made safe by a badge or disclaimer.
It must be routed into a supported shape, kept visibly uncertified only where
policy permits, or refused.

## Four correctness properties

"Zero factual errors" is useful as a north star only after it is decomposed.
The project treats these as separate properties because one can hold while
another fails:

| Property | Required meaning | Present posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Support correctness** | Every committed value is entailed by the certified snapshot; derived values are recomputed rather than trusted. | Structural invariant at the governed commit boundary. |
| **Applicability correctness** | The claim is about the right entity, profile, version, jurisdiction and effective time. | Structural where scope and subject are bound; ambiguous subject linking remains an explicit research surface. |
| **Answer correctness** | The committed answer satisfies the question actually asked, not merely a nearby question with individually true claims. | Measured by subject- and shape-aware oracles today. A typed, recorded question contract is the next structural target. |
| **Completeness correctness** | Every material qualification, comparison member, warning, contraindication or disclosure required for that answer is present. | Structural for encoded roster and disclosure obligations; limited by the completeness of the policy and claim schemas. |

A certified non-sequitur is therefore not counted as a fabrication, but it is
still a failed answer. A true value about the wrong product, patient, share
class or effective date may be more dangerous than an obvious invention; the
scorecard must not let support correctness conceal an applicability failure.

## The scorecard

Enforcement and usefulness never share a denominator.

### Current hard zeros

- unsupported governed claims committed;
- wrong-scope commits where the material profile is bound;
- missing mandatory qualifications on a committed artifact;
- unauthorized consequential actions executed; and
- records that cannot reproduce their original verdict under their pinned
  knowledge and policy versions.

These are invariants. They have no error budget and take no majority vote
across repetitions. A single occurrence falsifies the corresponding claim.
Wrong-subject commitments join this list only where the subject has been
structurally bound. Until a typed question contract makes that universal,
unbound or ambiguous subject relevance remains an empirical target below, not
a structural zero claimed in advance.

### Coverage and usefulness

- **knowledge coverage** — the share of representative in-scope demand the
  certified substrate and closed claim vocabulary are capable of answering;
- **routing recall** — the share of answerable questions mapped to the correct
  typed question and claim shape;
- **relevant resolution rate** — the share answered with the requested facts
  about the intended subject;
- **correct abstention rate** — unsupported, prohibited and genuinely
  ambiguous questions that ask, route or refuse appropriately;
- **stable resolution floor** — entries resolved in every repetition; and
- **variance band** — the range produced by repeated runs under one recorded
  configuration, with provider failures reported separately.

The target is not maximum answering. It is the best stable coverage attainable
without spending any of the hard zeros.

## Trusted base and explicit non-claims

The kernel cannot make a designated source true. A digest proves identity and
integrity, not correctness. Human certification can be wrong; an authoritative
document can be stale; a schema can omit a safety-critical qualification; a
bug can violate its specification; and pixels outside the verified rendering
boundary can mislead. Those are assurance obligations owned by source
designation, expert review, freshness policy, schema review, software testing
and platform controls respectively.

The demo therefore does not claim:

- universal truth outside its certified snapshot;
- complete coverage of the Pokémon domain, much less a regulated industry;
- that a label cures a false or misleading statement;
- that eligibility is the same as good advice; or
- that rates measured in this domain transfer to another one.

What is intended to transfer is the architecture and operating discipline.
Every new domain must re-earn its own envelope, hard zeros and usefulness
numbers.

## Falsification criteria

The thesis is weakened or falsified if any of the following occurs inside the
declared envelope:

1. a model-originated value reaches the governed surface without deterministic
   verification;
2. an unsupported, stale, wrong-subject or wrong-scope claim commits where the
   envelope says that applicability has been structurally bound;
3. a required material qualification is absent or not verifiably visible;
4. an action executes without continuity from the exact artifact the principal
   saw and confirmed;
5. replay under the pinned record produces a different verdict; or
6. useful coverage can be obtained only by weakening an enforcement invariant.

Finding one is a result, not a benchmark blemish. The responsible layer must
be repaired and the failure added to the permanent crucible or evaluation
bank.

## Research sequence

The adoption and research path is intentionally staged:

1. **Read-only governed knowledge.** State certified product, account,
   procedure or drug-label facts, or abstain honestly. Prove support,
   applicability, answer and completeness properties before adding agency.
2. **One real regulated-domain port.** Use a narrow, authoritative public
   source and measure not only answer quality but certification effort,
   freshness operations, schema changes and expert-review burden. This tests
   whether the architecture's economics generalize; a second Pokémon world
   cannot answer that question.
3. **Personalization and disclosure.** Add eligibility, jurisdiction and
   mandatory qualifications only after the read-only surface is stable.
4. **Consequential action.** Add consent and execution last, preserving the
   read-to-act proof identity already exercised by the demo.

The research hypothesis is thus precise: a small, model-agnostic commit kernel
can hold invalid governed commitments at zero while retrieval, routing and
models improve coverage upstream. The invariant is the product; the coverage
curve determines whether the product is useful.
